    How it works, and what is verified vs. assumed
    
    
      
  • Taken from the board photo: 60 track squares (15 per side, clockwise: yellow top, green right, red bottom, blue left). Each color's Start circle sits beside the 5th square of its side (the “exit square”), the Safety Zone lane turns off at the 3rd square of its side, and every side has two slides: a 4-square slide starting on the 2nd square and a 5-square slide starting on the 10th. Each Safety Zone has 5 squares and then Home.
    • 
      
  • Deck: 45 cards. Five 1s; four each of 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 8, 10, 11, 12 and Sorry!. The discard pile is reshuffled when the deck runs out.
    • 
      
  • A 1 or 2 brings a pawn out of Start onto your exit square (unless your own pawn is already there). A 2 always gives another draw, even if no move was possible.
    • 
      
  • Landing on an opponent's pawn on the main track sends it back to Start. Pawns in Safety Zones or Home can't be bumped. You can't land on your own pawn.
    • 
      
  • Slides: landing on the first square of a slide of another color sends the pawn to the end of the slide, and every pawn on the slide squares, including your own, goes back to Start. Slides of your own color do nothing. Slides apply to every kind of landing: normal moves, backward moves, 7 splits, 11 swaps (for both pawns) and Sorry!.
    • 
      
  • 4 moves back 4. 10 moves forward 10 or back 1. 7 moves one pawn 7 or splits between two pawns. 11 moves forward 11 or swaps with an opponent's main-track pawn. A backward move from near your exit square can carry a pawn behind it, close to your Safety Zone entrance.
    • 
      
  • Sorry! takes a pawn from Start and puts it on an opponent's main-track pawn, sending that one back to Start. With no pawn in Start or no target, the card is forfeited.
    • 
      
  • If no move is legal the card is forfeited. Home must be reached by exact count. Pawns in a Safety Zone can only move backward within it (assumption).
    • 
      
  • Smart player: looks at every legal move and picks the one that leaves the best position: more progress for itself, less for opponents, a penalty for pawns that opponents are likely to hit next turn, and a bonus for pawns that threaten opponents. Random player: picks any legal move at random. Mix them to test how much skill matters compared with seating. Real people may play differently.
    • 
      
  • Who goes first: “Always P1” follows the turn order shown (clockwise from the first selected color). “Random” picks the starting player each game, which separates the effect of seat position from the effect of moving first.
    • 
      
  • Games are split into fixed batches and simulated in background threads (when your browser supports it), so the page stays smooth. The same seed gives the same results on any device.
    • 
      
  • Games stop after 3,000 player turns and count as “no winner.”
